BEGINNER'S WALKTHROUGH

Your first day and week guide to running a successful ramen shop

📅 Day One: Opening Your Shop

Morning
Complete the tutorial prompts. Practice boiling noodles and assembling your first bowl. Don't worry about speed yet - focus on learning the controls.
Midday
Your first customers arrive. Start with simple orders - one ramen type only. Read customer reactions to learn preferences.
Evening
First rush hour. Keep calm, prioritize quality over speed. Close shop before you get overwhelmed to maintain morale.

💡 Early Game Priorities

1. Master One Recipe First

Don't try all recipes at once. Perfect one ramen type until you can make it consistently in under 2 minutes.

2. Stock Basic Ingredients

Always keep extra noodles, pork, and green onions. Running out mid-service is the fastest way to lose customers.

3. Watch Customer Patience

Each customer has a patience meter. If it depletes before serving, they leave AND hurt your reputation score.

4. Save Before Closing

The game auto-saves, but manually save after each successful service day to prevent progress loss.

👥 Customer Service Basics

Speed Matters

Average service time should be under 3 minutes. Faster service = higher tips and better reviews.

Accuracy Counts

Wrong toppings reduce satisfaction by 40%. Always confirm orders before cooking when unsure.

Fresh is Best

Serve immediately after assembly. Ramen sitting for 30+ seconds loses noodles firmness and broth temperature.

⬆️ Recommended Upgrade Order

  1. 1. Bigger Kettle - More simultaneous boiling capacity
  2. 2. Ingredient Storage Upgrade - Less restocking trips
  3. 3. Second Prep Station - Prepare toppings while cooking
  4. 4. Seating Expansion - More customers per service
  5. 5. Staff Hire - Assign roles to handle prep and service

⚠️ Common Beginner Mistakes

Overcooking Noodles

Noodles continue cooking after draining. Slightly undercook for perfect texture when served.

Ignoring Reputation

Low reputation means fewer customers. Monitor your rating and address complaints quickly.

Menu Overload

Start with 2-3 items. More menu options = more mistakes during rush hour.

No Backup Supplies

Always order ingredients before you run out. Emergency restocking costs 50% more.
